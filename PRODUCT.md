# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The owner and their household, on the home Wi-Fi. They reach for the controls
in two settings, about equally: on a phone in passing (near the panel or on the
sofa, often in the evening with the lights low) to skip a piece, hold one they
like, or dim the panel; and in a desktop browser tab while tinkering with the
firmware, to check status and try things. Each visit lasts a few seconds.

## Product Purpose

Pixel Jukebox is a 64x64 LED matrix that shuffles pixel art from Makapix Club.
Its interfaces (the page served by the board, and later a companion app) are
remote controls for it: see what is showing and who made it, skip, hold, set
brightness. Success is that the one thing you came to do is done in a tap, and
the artwork stays the centre of attention.

## Brand Personality

Playful, pixel-flavoured, warm. It should feel like a small handmade gadget
with character, closer to a jukebox than to a network device: chunky,
tactile controls and the artwork itself on show. Voice is plain and friendly,
with short labels and no jargon.

## Anti-references

- Router admin pages: grey tables, tiny links, default form controls.
- Generic AI dashboards: rounded cards in a grid, gradient accents, big hero
  numbers.

## Design Principles

1. The art is the show. The interface frames the artwork and never competes
   with it.
2. One tap to the thing you came for. Skip, hold and brightness are always
   visible; nothing lives behind menus.
3. Character without costume. Pixel flavour comes from shape, proportion and
   the artwork, not from piled-on retro effects.
4. Honest about the device. Show what the panel is really doing, including
   when it cannot be reached.
5. Small enough to live on the board. No external assets, and it works with
   no internet connection.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard good practice: readable contrast, touch targets of at least 44px,
full keyboard and screen-reader operation, and state never shown by colour
alone. It is often used in a dark room, so the default theme is dark and
avoids large bright areas. Motion is minimal and respects the system's
reduced-motion setting.
